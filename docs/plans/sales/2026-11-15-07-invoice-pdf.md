## 1. In one minute
After this plan an issued invoice can be handed out as an A4 PDF in the drawn format: the company's logo, full name, address and GSTIN in the header (repeated on every page), the school's details, the lines with no per-line GST column, a **rate-wise GST summary**, the totals and the amount in words, the bank details, the terms and conditions and the signature at the bottom, and `Page x of y`. Try it: `GET /api/v1/invoices/{id}/pdf` opens the PDF in the browser.

## 3. Approach
The PDF is built from the invoice and its snapshot (R28): when an invoice is made it also snapshots the company's printed identity — name, address, GSTIN, bank account, terms and footer (D7, R33) — so a reprint never changes when the company later edits its profile; the logo and signature are read live (cosmetic). The document is **HTML + CSS** rendered by **Scriban** (already in the solution) into the drawn A4 layout, then handed to `IHtmlToPdfRenderer` (platform#09) with A4, small margins, `printBackground`, a repeating header and a footer template carrying the document number, the bank details and `Page x of y` (R30). The GST is summed **by rate/HSN** into the summary rows, never shown per line (R32, D7); the computation is untouched (R7). The layout (R26) is chosen by the `?layout=class|booklist` parameter or the school's default. The PDF is rendered on demand and never stored (Q18). Amount in words is one domain helper in Indian numbering.

## 7. Risks and notes
The rendered PDF is verified by eye and by `%PDF`/page count, not by pixel diff. The company snapshot is additive; existing invoices (none in production) would have empty identity columns. The template's CSS is what keeps the layout compact; the renderer's A4 and margin options must match it.
